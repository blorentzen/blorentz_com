## T-MOBILE SAVINGS CALCULATOR (`tmobile-savings-calculator.ts`)

### 5.1 — Problem, Opening

**CURRENT:**
```
In late 2022, T-Mobile had a simple comparison chart showing pricing differences between carriers. It did the job, but it was static — a snapshot that couldn't adapt to the constantly shifting landscape of plan pricing, promotions, and competitive positioning. When a new value campaign launched in mid-2023, the business saw an opportunity to turn this static chart into something more powerful: an interactive tool that could position T-Mobile as the pricing and value leader in the marketplace.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
In late 2022, T-Mobile had a simple comparison chart showing pricing differences between carriers. It worked, but it was static — a snapshot that went stale every time plans changed. When a new value campaign launched in mid-2023, leadership saw an opportunity: turn the chart into an interactive tool that made the case for T-Mobile as the value leader.
```

**Why:** "constantly shifting landscape of plan pricing, promotions, and competitive positioning" is AI padding. The second sentence is too many words for a simple idea — cut it in half.

### 5.2 — Problem, Second Paragraph

**CURRENT:**
```
The challenge wasn't just building a calculator. It was building a tool that could evolve — from a basic line selector, to a benefits comparison engine, to a full savings calculator that computed pricing, savings percentages, and value breakdowns — all while remaining accurate, legally compliant, and simple enough for any customer to use.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
The real challenge: building something that could evolve. It started as a line selector, became a benefits comparison, and eventually turned into a full calculator that computed savings percentages and dollar amounts — all while staying accurate, legally compliant, and simple enough for anyone to use.
```

**Why:** "The challenge wasn't just X. It was Y." is the most common AI framing pattern.

### 5.3 — Approach, Opening

**CURRENT:**
```
This tool didn't start with a grand plan. It started in market and evolved through real-world insights. Each iteration was informed by data from the previous version — what customers clicked, where they dropped off, what questions they still had after using it.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
There was no grand plan. The tool started in market and each version was shaped by data from the last one — what people clicked, where they dropped off, what questions they still had.
```

**Why:** Two sentences doing the work of one. "real-world insights" is corporate filler.

### 5.4 — Approach, AEM Paragraph

**CURRENT:**
```
Due to the limitations of T-Mobile's CMS platform (Adobe Experience Manager) and the access constraints the team operates under, the entire tool was built with HTML, CSS, and Vanilla JavaScript — no frameworks. Everything was handwritten.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
T-Mobile's CMS (Adobe Experience Manager) is locked down — limited access, limited flexibility. So the entire tool was built with HTML, CSS, and Vanilla JS. No frameworks. Everything handwritten.
```

**Why:** "Due to the limitations of... and the access constraints the team operates under" is passive corporate writing. Just say it's locked down.

### 5.5 — Approach, Growth Paragraph

**CURRENT:**
```
Over time, the calculator grew beyond a single-use module. It became a dynamic placement that showcases the total value — benefits, perks, and pricing — that customers get when they sign up for a particular plan. The module now lives on the How to Switch page, the Plans page, and is deployed across multiple savings and value campaigns in constant advertising rotation. It's one of the only web modules in T-Mobile's history to be featured in a nationally broadcast TV commercial — including spots with the cast of Suits and Snoop Dogg.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Over time it grew past a single module. It's now a dynamic placement that breaks down the full value of a plan — benefits, perks, pricing — and lives on the How to Switch page, the Plans page, and across multiple ad campaigns in constant rotation. It's one of the only web modules in T-Mobile's history to appear in a nationally broadcast TV spot — including commercials with the Suits cast and Snoop Dogg.
```

**Why:** Minor tightening. "showcases the total value" → "breaks down the full value." "is deployed across multiple savings and value campaigns in constant advertising rotation" is too many words.

### 5.6 — Approach, Final Line

**CURRENT:**
```
I served as lead designer, lead developer, and effectively became the product owner of this tool — attending meetings with senior leadership to build and maintain the product roadmap.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
I was the lead designer, lead developer, and eventually the de facto product owner — in rooms with senior leadership building the product roadmap.
```

**Why:** "I served as" is resume voice. "attending meetings with" is passive. "In rooms with" is more direct.

### 5.7 — Result

**CURRENT:**
```
The metrics that turned the calculator into the centerpiece of T-Mobile's current advertising strategy:\n\n50% lift quarter-over-quarter in shop traffic to cart start rate (28.94% → 43.42%). 28% lift quarter-over-quarter in shop traffic to order rate (1.07% → 1.37%). Both metrics attributed to prospects who used the calculator before continuing down the funnel. Featured in nationally broadcast TV commercial spots (Suits cast, Snoop Dogg). Deployed across multiple pages and advertising campaigns in constant rotation. One of the only web modules to ever appear in a T-Mobile broadcast commercial.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
The numbers that made the calculator the centerpiece of T-Mobile's current ad strategy:\n\n50% lift quarter-over-quarter in shop-to-cart rate (28.94% → 43.42%). 28% lift in shop-to-order rate (1.07% → 1.37%). Both attributed to prospects who used the calculator before continuing down the funnel.\n\nNow featured in nationally broadcast TV spots — including commercials with the Suits cast and Snoop Dogg — and deployed across multiple pages and campaigns in constant rotation. One of the only web modules to ever appear in a T-Mobile broadcast commercial.
```

**Why:** Current version is a run-on list. Breaking it into metrics and context makes both hit harder. "shop traffic to cart start rate" → "shop-to-cart rate."

## FN5GL (`fn5gl.ts`)

### 6.1 — Problem, Opening

**CURRENT:**
```
Friday Night 5G Lights is one of T-Mobile's largest community-focused programs — a national sweepstakes where thousands of high schools across the country compete for a $1M+ grand prize package including a field makeover, Gronk Fitness gym equipment (contributed by Rob Gronkowski), and more.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Friday Night 5G Lights is a massive T-Mobile program — a national sweepstakes where thousands of high schools compete for a $1M+ prize package: field makeover, Gronk Fitness gym equipment from Rob Gronkowski, trip to the SEC Championship, the works.
```

**Why:** "one of T-Mobile's largest community-focused programs" is corporate-speak. "the works" is more Britton.

### 6.2 — Problem, In-house Transition

**CURRENT:**
```
In Year 1, much of the digital experience was handled by external agencies. For Year 2, the business wanted to own the core components — a dynamic leaderboard, an interactive school map, a school directory, and all the animations and design patterns — built to work within T-Mobile's AEM platform. This meant coordinating with multiple agencies that were handling program data while building the front-end experience internally.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Year 1 was mostly handled by external agencies. For Year 2, the goal was to bring the core experience in-house — dynamic leaderboard, interactive school map, school directory, all the animations — built to run in AEM. That meant coordinating with the agencies that still owned the data while we built the front-end internally.
```

**Why:** "the business wanted to own the core components" is corporate passive voice.

### 6.3 — Approach, Opening

**CURRENT:**
```
The scope of this project was massive. It involved multiple phases — pre-registration, registration, and the competition itself — each with distinct digital needs.\n\nThe core technical challenge was making the content as dynamic as possible.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
This was a big one. Multiple phases — pre-registration, registration, then the competition itself — each with different digital needs.\n\nThe core challenge was making everything as dynamic as possible.
```

**Why:** "The scope of this project was massive" is AI padding. "This was a big one" is how you'd actually say it. "distinct" is an AI tell.

### 6.4 — Approach, Contractor Story

**CURRENT:**
```
My role was Technical Lead: I hired and guided a front-end developer contractor for the majority of the project, provided design and development direction for all dynamic components, and coordinated with the API agency on data integration. About 60-70% of the way through, the contractor left to take a full-time position elsewhere. I picked up from where he left off and finished building all the dynamic components myself.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
I was the Technical Lead — hired and managed a front-end contractor, provided design and dev direction for all the dynamic components, and coordinated with the API agency on data integration. About 60-70% through, the contractor left for a full-time role. So I picked up where he left off and finished building everything myself.
```

**Why:** "My role was Technical Lead:" reads like a resume. "So I picked up" adds the causal link that makes it feel like a story being told.

### 6.5 — Result

**CURRENT:**
```
2,000+ schools applied from towns of 150,000 people or fewer across 48 states. 8M+ total votes cast during the competition. 450 schools won $5,000 through the $5K Fridays sweepstakes. 25 finalists each received $25,000. 1 grand prize winner (Dierks High School, Arkansas) scored a prize package worth over $1 million. Significant national press coverage. All dynamic components (leaderboard, map, directory, voting) built in-house and functioning within AEM. Program renewed for 2026 season.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
2,000+ schools applied across 48 states. 8M+ votes cast. 450 schools took home $5K through the weekly sweepstakes, 25 finalists got $25K each, and Dierks High School in Arkansas won the whole thing — a prize package worth over $1 million.\n\nEvery dynamic component — leaderboard, map, directory, voting — built in-house and running in AEM. Program renewed for 2026.
```

**Why:** Current is eight facts with periods between them. Grouping the 450 → 25 → 1 progression into a narrative arc makes it read like a story. "Significant national press coverage" is vague — cut it.

## DR. PATEL (`dr-patel-redesign.ts`)

### 7.1 — Problem, Second Paragraph

**CURRENT:**
```
In elective medical procedures, the website IS the first impression. Patients research extensively — looking at credentials, before-and-after galleries, reviews from other patients — before they'll even consider booking a consultation. Without a site that built confidence and showcased his work, Dr. Patel had no direct channel to the patients who were actively looking for what he offered. Every prospect had to go through the practice, where the routing problem was costing him business.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
In elective procedures, the website is the first consultation. Patients look at credentials, before-and-afters, and reviews before they'll even pick up the phone. Without his own site, every prospect had to go through the practice — where the routing problem kept costing him business.
```

**Why:** "the website IS the first impression" (caps IS) is trying too hard. "the website is the first consultation" is a better framing. "who were actively looking for what he offered" is padding.

### 7.2 — Approach, Opening

**CURRENT:**
```
This was a ground-up custom build — creating an entirely new digital presence from scratch that gave Dr. Patel a direct channel to prospective patients, bypassing the practice's broken routing entirely.\n\nThe strategy centered on tracking and understanding how prospects actually behave when researching plastic surgery.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Ground-up custom build. The whole point was giving Dr. Patel a direct channel to patients that bypassed the practice's broken routing.\n\nThe core idea was tracking how prospects actually behave when they're researching plastic surgery.
```

**Why:** "creating an entirely new digital presence from scratch" restates "ground-up custom build." "The strategy centered on" → "The core idea was" (simpler).

### 7.3 — Approach, Personalization

**CURRENT:**
```
I also built a lightweight version of personalization using JavaScript web components and URL parameters. When a prospect visited a procedure page and then navigated to the gallery or testimonials, the results were pre-filtered to show relevant content for the procedure they'd been researching. This created a guided journey — the site anticipated what the prospect wanted to see next.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
I also built lightweight personalization using JS web components and URL parameters. Visit a procedure page, then go to the gallery — the results are already filtered for that procedure. The site remembers what you were looking at.
```

**Why:** "This created a guided journey — the site anticipated what the prospect wanted to see next" is marketing-speak for a URL parameter. The rewrite uses second person, which is more vivid and half the words.

### 7.4 — Result

**CURRENT:**
```
Within the first year of the website going live:\n\n4,200+ unique visitors, with 1,800+ coming from Google Search. 22.6% engagement rate from the custom interactive modules. 200+ consultation inquiries via phone call, email, and form submissions. $500K+ estimated revenue realized from the new digital presence. The website is still in use today, now maintained by another agency after Dr. Patel was recruited by a practice in Bellevue.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
First year after launch:\n\n4,200+ unique visitors (1,800+ from Google). 22.6% engagement rate on the interactive modules. 200+ consultation inquiries across phone, email, and forms. An estimated $500K+ in revenue from the new site.\n\nDr. Patel was eventually recruited by a practice in Bellevue. The site got handed to their agency — and it's still running.
```

**Why:** "$500K+ estimated revenue realized from the new digital presence" is corporate. Bellevue detail becomes a story beat instead of a tacked-on sentence.

## CASCADEDS (`cascadeds.ts`)

### 8.1 — Problem Opening & Architecture Decisions

Keep as-is. The problem opening is specific and direct. The architecture section ("Tokens first, components second," etc.) reads like real decisions — least AI-sounding content on the site.

### 8.2 — Problem, Middle

**CURRENT:**
```
Most design systems assume you have a team — dedicated design ops, a component library maintainer, consumers who submit requests through a backlog. That model doesn't work for a solo practitioner or a small team shipping fast.\n\nBut the problem I was really trying to solve was bigger than consistency.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Most design systems assume you have a team — design ops, a library maintainer, consumers filing requests through a backlog. That doesn't work when you're one person shipping fast.\n\nBut the real problem was bigger than consistency.
```

**Why:** Minor tightening. Same meaning, fewer words.

### 8.3 — Approach, "Why build your own?"

**CURRENT:**
```
Why build your own? The systems that exist weren't built for how I work. Tailwind creates class soup at scale and doesn't enforce design decisions. Material UI and Chakra impose opinions that don't match my aesthetic or my clients' brands. None of them give me a single source of truth that spans both my design tool and my code, and none of them bridge applications and marketing pages.\n\nCascadeDS embodies a philosophy I apply to client work: own the things that define your quality. When a client hires Empac, they get a system-driven approach — not a one-off design that falls apart when someone adds a page.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
Why build your own? Nothing out there fit how I work. Tailwind creates class soup at scale. Material UI and Chakra impose their opinions on your brand. None of them bridge applications and marketing pages, and none give me one source of truth across Figma and code.\n\nIt comes down to a principle I apply to client work too: own the things that define your quality. Hire Empac and you get a system — not a one-off design that falls apart when someone adds a page.
```

**Why:** "The systems that exist weren't built for how I work" → "Nothing out there fit" (shorter). "CascadeDS embodies a philosophy" → "It comes down to a principle" (less grand).

### 8.4 — Approach, Speed Section

**CURRENT:**
```
The biggest practical benefit is building things faster so products ship to market quicker. Every new project starts with a working foundation instead of blank-file decisions. That's the difference between spending a week on setup versus spending it on the actual problem.\n\nWhen Empac grows, CascadeDS provides the rails. A new contributor installs the package, references the tokens, and the system enforces consistency automatically.
```

**PROPOSED:**
```
The practical upside: speed. Every new project starts with a working foundation instead of a blank file. That's a week spent on the actual problem instead of setup.\n\nWhen Empac grows, CascadeDS is the rails. New contributor installs the package, references the tokens, consistency happens automatically.
```

**Why:** "building things faster so products ship to market quicker" says the same thing twice. "provides the rails" → "is the rails" (tighter).
